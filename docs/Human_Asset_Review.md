# The people of the game, from every side (15 November 2026)

Asked for: a full review of every human 3D figure, photographed from the front, the back and the side, and in the three play views (from above, from behind, through the eyes), set against other 3D games, with the issues found. Nothing in the game was changed.

The pictures and charts are on the review page: https://claude.ai/artifact/7pKRpZ763qEY1xN9yrAvzK (private to its owner until shared). The tools that made them are in `tools/review/`. This review measures the figures afresh; where it differs from `Figure_Comparison.md` (which said "about six heads tall"), this one is the later and the more exact.

## In short

The cast is complete and well animated, and its cloth is rich in pattern. Its fault is sameness.

One strongman body, one stance, one crown and one outline are shared by nearly everyone. The 3D people do not look like the game's own paintings of them. Cloth hangs as boards, children are small adults, and five people, your friend Vasu among them, are still the old peg dolls.

The faults of fit that a program could count (skin through cloth, hollow armpits, knees in the ground) are real, and smaller.

Since the review, one thing has been decided: the artist's sheets are the true look. Set beside those sheets, the models keep their colours and little else.

## What was looked at

- **154 costumes** on 79 modelled people, each photographed from the front, the side and behind
- **79 faces** close, from three sides; then hands, feet, hair and cloth
- **13 poses** on eight kinds of figure, and 41 other story people and crowd figures
- **79 bodies** measured as with a tape, beside a survey of 6,068 real people
- **43,428 views** checked by program: each costume in 47 poses from six sides
- **3 fights** played on a phone-size screen in each of the three views

## Decided: the artist's sheets are the true look

On 15 November the owner chose: the artist's sheets are the true look. So the test of a model is its sheet. Six people were set beside their sheets, and the sixteen heroes' war dress beside theirs. The models keep the sheets' colours, who is crowned and who is in armour, and the order of heights. They do not yet keep the rest.

What the sheets have that the models lack:

- **Each hero's own war dress.** The sheets give every hero a coat, a crown and a sign of his own. The models give all of them one coat of scales in a tint.
- **Cloth.** The sheets wind the lower cloth in pleats and lay the upper cloth in folds; each sheet has a diagram of the winding. The models have breeches and planks.
- **A body for each.** Arjuna and Bhima are drawn with a hero's bulk at about 7½ heads, Yudhishthira lean, Bhima as a child about 5 heads tall and soft, the women with a woman's hips and waist.
- **Heads.** Hair in wavy locks, lids resting on the eyes, moustaches and beards with form. Some beards differ outright: the sheet gives Yudhishthira a moustache and Duryodhana a moustache and chin beard; the models give the first a full beard and the second none.
- **Small things.** Sandals on several at war, Arjuna's two quivers, far more jewellery.

## Done since the review

Version 56 of the game, published the same day, takes the first small steps. Each was checked with the same pictures and counts as the review.

- **Eyes at rest.** The upper lid now lies on the iris, as on the sheets. The stare is gone from every face.
- **Skin is matte**, and the light on gold coats is smaller and tighter, so chests no longer burn out to white.
- **Knees stay above the ground.** A pleading figure now goes 6 cm under where it went 7.4: its feet still dig in, which is left to do.
- A move started without a length no longer breaks the figure.

## Findings, the most seen first

### 1. The fights show different people from the story

The story screens show the painted portraits. The fights show the 3D cast. Laid side by side they are two sets of people.

- Twelve people have both a painted portrait and a 3D head. Only Drona, Bhishma and Mauni are plainly the same man in both.
- In paint: lean bodies of real proportion, matte skin, cloth in folds, bare heads or a low band. In 3D: a strongman's build, glossy skin, hair in beads, a tall tiered crown.
- Arjuna, Bhima and Karna are bare-headed in paint and crowned in 3D. Yudhishthira wears a thin gold band in paint and the tall crown in 3D. Bhima has stubble in paint and a handlebar moustache in 3D. Duryodhana has a moustache in paint and none in 3D. Ghatotkacha has hair and a horned crown in paint and is bald in 3D. Satyaki is mid-brown in paint and near-black in 3D.
- The 3D cast was built from the artist's sheets; the portraits were painted earlier, for the 2D game. **Decided on 15 November: the artist's sheets are the true look.**

Others: Hades is also a painted story with 3D fighters. Its makers sculpt each 3D head against the 2D portrait, laid over it until the two agree ([Supergiant on Hades' characters](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/learn-how-supergiant-brought-i-hades-i-hand-painted-characters-to-life)).

A fix (decided: the sheets): With the sheets as the true look, the paintings were the ones to change: 18 of the 31 portraits and nine story paintings (the [repaint brief](Repaint_Brief.md) names them). The 18 portraits have since been cut from the sheets themselves, with no painter (Version 57); the story paintings stay as they are, by the owner's choice. But the models are not yet the sheets either: see [the sheets beside the models](#sheets). Some of the gap was made on 12 November, when beards and hair were set to the paintings: Yudhishthira's sheet gives him a moustache, his painting and his model a full beard.

### 2. One strongman body is worn by everyone

Villager, priest, prince, grandfather and child stand on the same frame: very wide shoulders, thick upper arms and neck, a small waist. Only the bulk changes. The artist's sheets give each person a body: Arjuna and Bhima a hero's bulk, Yudhishthira a lean one, a child a child's, a woman a woman's.

- **Shoulders.** Our middle grown man is 0.374 of his height across the shoulders. In the US Army's survey of 4,082 men the average is 0.291 and the widest man is 0.364. Our slimmest grown man, Sahadeva, is broader than 99.5% of them.
- **Upper arms.** 60 cm round on a 1.86 m man. A soldier of that height averages 38 cm, and the thickest arm in the survey would be 52 cm. Bhima's is 89 cm. The villager, the brahmin and the bard have the thinnest arms among our grown men: 46 to 47 cm, thicker than 99.8% of the soldiers.
- **The balloon.** The upper arm is 1.97 times the forearm in girth (2.4 times on women and children). In life it is 1.15 times, and the largest ratio among 4,082 men is 1.38.
- **Waist.** Chest to waist is 1.42 on our men; soldiers average 1.13 and 99 in 100 are under 1.30.
- **Old men** have the same body with white hair. Blind old Dhritarashtra is 73 cm across the shoulders with 58 cm arms. Nobody stoops; nobody is thin, soft or fat.
- **Women** share one body: the nine female models past childhood all measure the same. Its shoulders are 1.57 times its hips; women in the survey average 1.28, men 1.48. Its waist is 51 cm at 1.69 m, when the smallest waist among 1,986 women would be 65 cm at that height. Its forearm is 15 cm round against 27 cm in life.
- **One skeleton.** Thigh to shin, upper arm to forearm and hip height are the same to three decimal places on all 79 models. No one is long-legged, short-armed or high-shouldered.
- Heads are large on purpose, to read small from above: 6.4 heads tall. The sheets draw about 7½, as in life.

Others: 0 A.D., an open game set in the same India, gives its Mauryan soldiers lean bodies of ordinary build and tells them apart by turban, shield and dress (pictures further down). Hades starts most characters from one of two base bodies and reshapes each ([Supergiant on Hades' characters](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/learn-how-supergiant-brought-i-hades-i-hand-painted-characters-to-life)). Team Fortress 2 and Overwatch give each role its own body before anything else ([Character readability in Team Fortress 2 and Overwatch](https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465)).

A fix (large): Five or six base bodies in place of one: a slight youth, an ordinary man, a heavy man (Bhima, Duryodhana, Ghatotkacha), an old man who stoops, a woman with her own hips, waist and forearms, and a child. Each is taken from its sheet, with the survey as a check on the numbers. Every garment is then fitted again by the builder, which already fits them by program.

### 3. The heroes share one outline, one crown and one coat

In war dress the side reads at once: Pandava gold and silver, Kaurava black and brown. The man does not.

- Of the 22 named heroes with a war costume, 13 wear the same tall tiered crown. 20 wear the same coat of scales with the same shoulder plates and bracers, and 17 of those the same greaves; all wear the same puffed breeches. Only Ghatotkacha goes bare-chested.
- As black shapes, scaled to one height: Dhrishtadyumna and Shalya overlap by 96%, Nakula and Yudhishthira by 93%, Karna and Jayadratha by 92%, Bhima and Duryodhana by 90%. Those last two must be told apart in the mace duel that ends the war. Every one of the 22 has another hero within 86%.
- From behind they are one man: bead hair, crown, the back of the coat, breeches with a centre seam.
- No model carries a sign, though the sheets give one to nearly every hero: a lion on Bhima's chest, shoulders, belt and greaves, a horse's head on Nakula, a bull's on Sahadeva, the sun on Karna, flames on Dhrishtadyumna's shoulders. The [table of sheets and models](#sheets) lists all sixteen. No one has a quiver, a shield or a sword at rest on back or hip either.
- Bhima at home and Krishna at home wear the same yellow breeches and red belt. Yellow silk is Krishna's own mark.

Others: Valve's guide for Dota 2 heroes begins with a clear silhouette, then value, then colour ([Valve's character art guide for Dota 2, as reported](https://www.cgchannel.com/2012/06/valve-releases-useful-character-design-guide)). The same test, the black shape, is how Team Fortress 2's nine classes are told apart ([Character readability in Team Fortress 2 and Overwatch](https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465)). Shadow Fight 3 wanted each faction to read at first glance ([The making of Shadow Fight 3](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-making-of-shadow-fight-3/)).

A fix (medium): Build each hero's war dress from his own sheet: his crown, his coat (scales, plate, black and gold), his sign on the chest and shoulders, his shawl or sash. Add a mark on the back for the view from behind: a quiver for the archers, a slung shield. All of this sits on the present bodies.

### 4. Cloth hangs as boards, and everyone wears breeches

Patterns and woven borders are the best thing on these figures. The cloth they are drawn on is stiff.

- Every upper cloth is a flat strip across the chest and a rectangular panel that hangs like a plank. From the side it stands clear of the body.
- Kunti's veil is a slab behind her head. Draupadi's bridal veil is a red ball on the back of hers.
- In mourning, the costume of the last act, all eight people wear the same white breeches and the same three planks.
- Every lower cloth, on men and women alike, is the same pair of puffed breeches. Draupadi's sari is breeches with planks: no wrapped skirt, no pleats, no fall over the shoulder. Women's long breeches are crumpled behind the knees.
- The planks stay rigid in movement. When Draupadi squats or kneels, hers stands upright through her lap.
- The forest costume's deer hide is a patch close to skin colour with pale spots. On fair Nakula and Sahadeva it reads as a rash.

Others: Every artist's sheet carries a diagram of how the lower cloth is wound, and draws the upper cloth in folds. 0 A.D. wraps the lower cloth with pleats and a striped waist-cloth and lays one upper cloth over a shoulder. Raji's makers took dress and line from Pahari miniature painting ([Raji: An Ancient Epic, its makers on the art](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d/magazine/raji-an-ancient-epic-enter-the-realm-of-indian-mythology.html)).

A fix (large): Model the lower cloth as a wrapped garment with a pleated fall in front and the tucked tail behind, and a true skirt wrap for women. Model the upper cloth lying on the shoulder and chest with some thickness and two or three folds, its free end on the swinging joints the figures already have. This goes with the new bodies of finding 2: do them together.

### 5. Children and youths are small adults

A child here is the grown skeleton at two-thirds size.

- Our children stand 6.7 heads tall and our grown men 6.4, so the children have the smaller heads for their height. It should be the other way: a child of eight is about 6 to 6¼ heads, an adult 7.5.
- For their height, the children's shoulders are wider than 97% of grown soldiers' and their upper arms thicker than 95%. Bhima as a child (1.37 m) is 52 cm across the shoulders with 38 cm arms, thicker than an average grown soldier's (36 cm).
- All 14 children share very nearly one face: an adult's cheekbones and long jaw, with dark rings round the eyes.
- Most of the youths share one hair knot and nearly one face, the adult face without its beard. They do not look fourteen.
- The player as a child has a war costume: a coat of scales and a helmet on a child of eight.

Others: Drawing manuals put a child of eight at about 6¼ heads and a twelve-year-old at 7 ([Measuring the child figure with the head (US Navy illustrator's manual)](https://draftingmanuals.tpub.com/14262/css/Measuring-The-Child-Figure-With-The-Head-149.htm); [Drawing different ages: heads tall by age](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/human-anatomy-fundamentals-drawing-different-ages--cms-21905)). A child's shoulders are narrow and level because the muscles that slope an adult's are not grown yet.

A fix (medium to large): A child's body and face as the sheets draw them: Bhima's child sheet is about 5 heads tall, round-faced and soft, where his model is 6.7 heads and muscled. A lighter body for youths.

### 6. One stare, painted beards, bead hair, waxy skin

The named grown-ups do have faces of their own: jaw, nose and brow differ, and Bhima, Krishna, Duryodhana and Drona are known at once. What they share spoils it.

- **Eyes.** One eye for all: round, wide, the white showing all the way round a small iris. The upper lid never rests on the iris, so everyone looks startled. It is worst on the woman player, Charu, the brahmin, Nakula and Sahadeva.
- **Beards.** On the younger men a beard is a dark zone painted flat on the jaw with a straight cut edge. From three-quarters it reads as a chin-guard (Ashwatthama, the bard, the raider, the foot soldier, Satyaki, Yudhishthira). The old men's beards and Bhima's moustache have real volume.
- **Hair.** Glossy bunches of black beads beside the ears on every prince, a smooth cap above.
- **Skin.** A light band and a dark band cross every cheek. It reads as wax, and on youths as hollow cheeks. Gold armour burns out to white on the chest and shoulders.
- **Hands and feet.** A hand on a weapon is good: the fist closes round the haft. An empty hand hangs as a curled paw with no fingers. Feet have no toes; they are smooth socks, 12% shorter than in life.
- The brahmin is bald with no tuft of hair at the crown.

Others: On the sheets the lids rest on the iris, hair falls in wavy locks, and beards and moustaches have form. Hades paints brows, lids and lips onto its heads over baked shading ([Supergiant on Hades' characters](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/learn-how-supergiant-brought-i-hades-i-hand-painted-characters-to-life)).

A fix (small to medium): Lower the lids and enlarge the iris (the joints for lids and brows exist). Soften the beard's edge or model it like the elders'. Hair as a few sculpted locks. Turn the skin's sheen down and narrow the highlight on gold. Cut four fingers and the toes into the present mesh.

### 7. Borrowed bodies, peg dolls, and marks that go missing

Beyond the 79 models, 37 more story people are made by borrowing a body or by the old code-built figure.

- **Five people are still peg dolls** with tube limbs, no hands and flat colour: Vasu, your friend through the whole life, and the four beings (gandharva, rakshasa, naga, yaksha). Beside the modelled cast they belong to another game.
- **Eight named warriors are one soldier:** Bhurishravas, Kritavarma, Vrishasena, Iravan, Yudhamanyu, Uttamaujas, Vikarna and the champion share one body, one silver coat and one bearded face, with a crest and a sash in another colour. Vrishasena and Iravan are youths in the story and bearded men here.
- **Old men come out young.** Haridatta at his dice-board and Sudatta are written as old and turbaned. They appear as young, bare-headed, black-bearded men, because there is no old villager model and a turban, a beard or an age set in the story is not put on a modelled body. The priest loses his matted hair and beard; Revat, your father and the townsmen lose their head-bands.
- Your mother, your father and every townsman and townswoman are one villager each way, in white with a thin coloured border.
- **Near and far disagree.** The plain figure that stands for a soldier far off is slim, with red cross-straps on a bare chest or a gold coat and crested helmet. Close up the same man is brawny in a brown vest and a steel cap.
- The plain far-off figures carry marks the modelled ones lack: the archer's quiver on his back, the cross-straps, the plumed helmet with cheek-pieces.

Others: 0 A.D. draws the same soldier near and far.

A fix (small to medium): Small: put headgear, beard and age marks on modelled bodies, and add one old villager. Medium: model Vasu at his ages and the four beings; give the warrior body four or five variants; make the far-off figure a thinned copy of the near one, or dress the two alike.

### 8. Faults of fit that a program could count

Each costume was put through 47 poses and looked at from six sides, in flat colours that say which part is which. These are the faults that showed, each then checked by eye.

- **Chest through the sash.** Standing still, a patch of chest shows through the upper cloth on 66 of 154 costumes: the court costumes with a sash over a bare chest. Duryodhana 138 cm², Bhima 130, Ghatotkacha 117, Dhritarashtra 100.
- The woman player at home shows a patch of belly through her white upper cloth.
- **Knees through the lower cloth** in a squat on 102 of 154 costumes, 120 to 220 cm² on the worst.
- **Raised arms.** When an arm goes up (aiming a bow, cheering, winding up a heavy blow) the armour's arm-hole shows its hollow inside on 34 costumes, and on bare bodies the shoulder folds in and the inside of the body shows at the armpit on 45.
- **The ground.** On every costume a kneeling figure's lower cloth goes 4.5 cm under the ground (the knee itself does not), and a pleading figure's feet and knees went 7.4 cm under. Standing, on guard, walking and running are all within 1 cm of the ground.
- Clean: the guards on forearm and shin, and the chest armour standing (the fix of Version 55 holds).
- One trap in the code: a move started without a length (`f.act("slash")`) fills every joint with an invalid number and the figure is gone for good. The game always gives a length; a default would make it safe.

A fix (small each): Each is a small change in the builder or the pose tables: let the sash ride on the chest, widen the lower cloth at the knee or hide the knee under it, close the arm-hole with a gusset, lift the kneeling poses.

### 9. Everyone stands and sits the same way

In movement the figures are at their best. At rest they are a row of copies.

- The standing pose holds both arms 20 to 30 degrees off the body, feet parallel, weight even. On broad men it reads as a strongman's pose. King, priest, child and queen all hold it.
- "Sit" is a deep squat on the toes with the arms held out, for everyone. People of this world sit cross-legged on the floor or on a low seat.
- A pose named "down" is an upright sprawl; the fall itself is done elsewhere and was not judged here.

A fix (small): Four or five ways to stand, by temper and age: weight on one leg, hands joined at the waist, an old man leaning on a staff, a child shifting. A cross-legged seat and a seat on a stool.

## In the three play views

The same moment of a fight, on a phone-size screen, in each of the three views. Each view shows a different side of the figures, so each is hurt by different findings.

**From above** (the view a fight opens in)

- A figure is about 75 pixels tall on a screen 844 high. Faces, patterns and the shape of a crown are gone at that size.
- What still reads: the colour of the lower cloth, the disc of a shield, the weapon, a dot of headgear. Weapons are drawn large on purpose (a mace as long as its bearer), and from here that is right.
- The ring on the ground says which figure is you. The figure does not.
- So this view needs finding 3 in its plainest form: one bold colour and one carried shape for each man. Builds, faces and stance do not show.

**From behind** (your own back, all fight long)

- Your back holds the middle of the screen: helmet, the back of the coat, breeches, bare calves. Nothing is carried on it.
- It is the least designed side of every figure, and from behind all the heroes are one man.
- The director of Stellar Blade gave the lead's back extra care because "the player is always facing the back of the character" ([his words](https://80.lv/articles/stellar-blade-s-game-director-on-the-importance-of-creating-visually-appealing-characters)).
- A quiver, a slung shield, a sash end or a braid would be seen here more than anywhere.

**Through your eyes** (the foe at arm's length)

- Nothing of you is on the screen: no forearms, no weapon. Your own blow cannot be seen, only what it does.
- A foe at arm's length fills the screen with his shield and his face. This is where the stare (finding 6), the planks of cloth (finding 4) and the hollow under a raised arm (finding 8) show most.
- Games fought in first person keep the hands and the weapon in view. Rust draws the ordinary body and moves its bones out of the camera's way ([how](https://pcgamer.com/witness-the-body-horror-that-allows-rust-players-to-look-down-and-see-their-legs)). The makers of Zeno Clash warn that in first person it is hard to judge whether a swing lands ([their notes](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/5-tips-for-first-person-face-punching)).
- A fix of medium size: draw your own forearms and weapon in this view.

## What is good, and should be kept

- The cast is whole and of one hand: 79 people, 154 costumes, none missing, one line round every figure.
- Woven borders and patterns on cloth, and the scale and plate work of the armour.
- Movement. On guard, a cut, a heavy blow, being struck, walking and running all read at a glance. Faces shout on a blow. A run has a true moment with both feet off the ground.
- The hand on a weapon: the fist closes round the haft.
- Soldiers' headgear tells their kind: turban, crest, steel cap, top-knot.
- Marks that work: Krishna's colour and peacock feather, Draupadi's loose hair, Gandhari's blindfold, Dhritarashtra's shut eyes, Karna's golden coat as a boy, Drona's top-knot and beads, the hermit's matted hair, Arjuna's braid in his year of disguise.
- Feet on the ground: standing, on guard, walking and running are within 1 cm.
- Weight: 10,500 to 19,100 triangles a figure, fit for a phone.

## How it was done, and what it could not do

- Pictures: a plain grey studio, one light, each figure turned square to the camera. Faces, hands, feet and cloth were taken closer the same way.
- Measures: each figure standing; girths taken by cutting the skin with a plane, as a tape lies, with skin under clothing included. The yardstick is the US Army's 2010-12 survey, whose tables are public ([US Army body survey 2010-12 (ANSUR II), about the data](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/); [The survey tables used here (a public copy)](https://github.com/hkair/anthropometric-stats)): 4,082 men and 1,986 women, fit and of mixed build. It stands for real fit people. It is a yardstick, and epic heroes may fairly be bigger than soldiers; a villager, a priest and a child may not.
- Head height in life (7.5 heads) is the artists' rule ([Body proportions: 7.5 heads for an adult](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Body_proportion)); the survey does not measure it.
- The scan: each costume in 47 poses, from front, back, both sides, above and behind-above, drawn once in flat part colours with depth and once with the skin left out. Skin counts as "through" when cloth meant to cover that part of the body lies within 5 cm behind it. Areas are as seen in one view. The twelve worst cases were then drawn normally and looked at.
- Outlines: each hero in war dress drawn as a black shape from the front and the side, scaled to one height; overlap is the shared area over the combined area.
- The sheets: after the decision, the artist's sheets (181 pictures) were opened again. Six people were set beside their models in full, and the sixteen heroes' war dress beside theirs. The other sheets have not yet been gone through one by one.
- Play views: three fights (a war day, the sparring trial, the chariot fight) played by the game's own fighter on a 390 by 844 screen, slowed, and captured in each view at the same moment.

Limits:

- Other games. I could open and study pictures from 0 A.D. (open art, the same period and place) and the game's own paintings. I could not look at pictures of commercial games here: the picture search shows its results to you and not to me, and no browser was linked. What is said of Hades, Team Fortress 2, Overwatch, Dota 2, Raji, Shadow Fight 3, Stellar Blade and Rust comes from what their makers have published, linked beside each claim.
- Movement was judged from stills, and sound not at all. Animals and chariots were outside this review.
- The far ranks and watchers are pegs of 222 to 500 triangles, as they should be at their distance; they were only checked against the soldier they stand for.
