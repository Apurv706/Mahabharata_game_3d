# Gameplay ideas for the 3D fights (12 November)

Asked for: the game was designed for 2D play; now that fights are 3D, how can they be made more interesting, and how should the divine weapons be invoked, land and be countered. Part 1 was built on 12 November (see the end of `docs/Conversion_Notes.md` for what was and was not done); the owner ruled that men who flee count as beaten up to a cap, that the counter is a contest, and that only a poor invocation changes the force. Part 2 is ideas only.

## Part 1. The divine weapons, in three beats

Today: you press the weapon's button, the fight freezes for 2.6 seconds, light gathers, the struck are thrown or burnt. A champion "answers with the counter-weapon" in one line of text and a flash.

### Beat 1. Invocation: a figure to join

- Pressing the button does not fire. The world slows to about a fifth, the camera comes down behind your shoulder, the sky darkens and the mantra's syllables rise.
- A yantra of five to seven points appears. You join the points in order with one stroke of the finger (keys: the numbers in order). That stroke is the code.
- Each weapon has its own figure, so the hand learns them: Agneya an upward triangle, Varuna a downward one with a wave, Vayavya a spiral, Sammohana a lotus of five, the great weapons a full star.
- How well you draw is how hard it lands: clean and quick, full force; slow or off the points, a weaker cast; broken, "the mantra broke" (the rule the game already has) and the weapon rests.
- Foes keep moving, slowly, while you draw. Invoking with a spear two paces away is a risk you chose.
- The story teaches the figure. When a teacher gives you the weapon you trace it once with him. Whoever was never taught it has no figure to draw.
- Curses bite here: a cursed mantra's points fade or change place as you draw (Karna's forgetting, played rather than told).
- For ease of play: a setting that draws it for you at reduced force, and it honours the calm-motion setting.

### Beat 2. Impact: it is seen, and men break

- The weapon travels. It leaves the bow or the hand, climbs, divides, and comes down where the foes stand, so you watch it go.
- Each lands as itself: fire stands in pillars, scorches the ground and men run burning; water comes as a wall and carries men, shields and banners with it; wind lifts and scatters; sleep drops a block of men where they stand.
- The ground remembers for the rest of the fight: scorch, mud, flattened standards.
- Panic. Soldiers near the strike throw down spears and run. The far ranks draw back from you. For some seconds no ordinary soldier will close with you, and your own men shout.
- A rule to settle: do men who flee count as beaten? Suggested: yes, they leave the fight alive, up to a cap, so the weapon clears a field without a slaughter and the balance bands can be kept.

### Beat 3. The counter: two weapons meet

- A champion does not simply cancel it. The camera cuts to him; his own yantra flashes; he looses the answer the epic gives him (fire met by water, wind by mountain, serpents by the eagle, sleep by waking).
- The two meet in the air between you, in view of both, and hold there.
- Optional contest: while they hold, you keep tracing your figure against a filling ring. Win and a part of yours gets through (he staggers); lose and you are thrown back. Draw and both are spent, as now.
- The meeting throws down the soldiers of both sides beneath it, and the sky clears.

## Part 2. What three dimensions allow that the 2D rules never used

Ordered by how much play each adds for the work.

1. **Morale.** Each host has a spirit you can see in its banners and hear in its drums. Felling a captain, cutting down a standard, sounding your conch or a divine weapon moves it. A host that breaks runs. It ties straight into the panic above.
2. **Facing and flanks.** A shield covers the front only. A blow from behind or the side lands harder and cannot be blocked. Your six orders start to matter: the wall protects flanks, the spread exposes them.
3. **Things to do other than kill everyone.** Hold the gap for a count; cut through to a fallen friend and bring him out; take the banner; guard your chariot's driver; reach the champion before he reaches your king.
4. **Ground that matters.** High ground lengthens a bow's reach; mud by the river slows; dust hides; a wrecked chariot is cover (the game already lets you take cover).
5. **Duels.** When two champions meet the soldiers ring them, the camera drops behind you, and it becomes a contest of guard and timing: wear down his guard, break it, finish. A conch is how you call one.
6. **Chariots as play, not scenery.** Shoot from a moving car; kill the driver or the horses to bring an archer down to your level; elephants charge down a marked lane you must leave.
7. **The bow from your own eyes.** Draw, hold, loose, with the arrow's fall to judge; called shots at a bowstring, a banner pole, a wheel.
8. **The mace's weight.** A slam that throws men down in a ring, a blow that breaks armour for those who follow you, a throw.
9. **Dharma inside the fight.** The moment a foe is down, unarmed or has turned his back, a choice appears for a breath: strike, spare, wait. The war remembers which you made.
10. **Hour and weather.** Fighting stops at dusk and the horn ends the fight where it stands; the night raid by torchlight; a dust storm.
11. **Wounds you carry.** A wound taken shows on your figure and costs you something specific until it is treated.
12. **The killing blow.** A brief slowing and a closer camera on the stroke that ends a champion.

## Suggested order

1. The divine weapons' three beats (asked for, and self-contained).
2. Morale and panic (the second beat needs it anyway).
3. Facing and flanks, then objectives.
4. Duels.
The rest as taste dictates.
